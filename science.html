The Science of Digestion

Decoding the Acronym

FODMAPs are a group of short-chain carbohydrates (sugars and fibers) that are poorly absorbed in the small intestine. Unlike other nutrients that are absorbed into the bloodstream, these molecules travel to the large intestine where they cause two specific mechanical issues: Osmotic Load and Rapid Fermentation.

Why Bloating Happens

It's not "all in your head." It is a physical reaction to physics and chemistry.

1. The Osmotic Effect

FODMAPs are "osmotically active," meaning they attract water. When they sit in the small intestine unabsorbed, they pull significant fluid into the bowel lumen. This increases volume and alters bowel motility (speed), often leading to diarrhea.

2. Bacterial Fermentation

When these carbohydrates reach the large intestine, gut bacteria rapidly ferment them. This produces hydrogen, carbon dioxide, and methane gases. Because the bowel is already filled with fluid (from Step 1), this gas has nowhere to go, stretching the intestinal wall and causing visceral pain.

Kitchen Chemistry

How Datatribute creates safe, flavorful menus using solubility and enzymatic principles.

The Garlic Oil Hack

The Science: Fructans (the trigger in garlic) are water-soluble, but not oil-soluble.


Our Method: We cannot use chopped garlic. Instead, we infuse extra virgin olive oil with whole garlic cloves, allowing the flavor compounds to transfer to the fat while the fructans remain trapped in the clove. We then strain the solids. The result: Full garlic flavor, Zero fructans.

The Sourdough Effect

The Science: Wheat is high in fructans, but traditional fermentation degrades them.


Our Method: We use a slow-fermentation sourdough process (minimum 24 hours). During this time, the lactobacilli and yeast cultures consume the fructans in the flour as fuel. By the time the bread is baked, the fructan content has dropped to safe, Monash-certified levels.

Our Data Protocol

We adhere to the 3-Phase approach developed by clinical researchers.

Phase 1: Elimination (The "Reset")

This is the standard for our Wedding & Corporate Catering. We remove ALL high-FODMAP triggers to ensure the menu is universally safe for every guest. This is the "lowest common denominator" of digestive safety.

Phase 2: Reintroduction (The "Challenge")

Used in our Meal Prep Subscription. We introduce specific groups (e.g., small amounts of avocado for sorbitol) to test individual tolerance thresholds.

Phase 3: Personalization (The "Algorithm")

Using our proprietary data models, we build a long-term menu for private clients that maximizes variety. If our data shows you tolerate GOS (legumes) but not Fructans (onions), we adjust your meals accordingly.
